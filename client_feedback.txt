1.⁠ ⁠Rewrite still changes facts in some cases
I tested a reply from Phone B saying “he’s been a nightmare, he told a teacher to fuck off” and the rewrite delivered as “He has been difficult. He told a teacher to leave him alone.” Softening “nightmare” to “difficult” is fine, that’s tone. But “told a teacher to fuck off” becoming “told a teacher to leave him alone” changes what the child actually did. The first is the child being rude and swearing at a teacher. The second is the child setting a boundary.
I undnAI’s constraints and that prompts can shape behaviour but not guarantee it. Worth tightening the rewrite prompt to be more explicit about the difference between softening tone and changing facts. Suggested addition, but happy to have your input:

PRESERVE FACTS, SOFTEN ONLY TONE: When a message describes something a child or third party actually did or said, preserve the action accurately. You may soften descriptive adjectives (“nightmare” → “difficult”) but you must NOT change the action itself. Verbs describing what someone did, said, or what happened are FACTS, not tone. If the action contains profanity or aggression, keep the action and soften only the surrounding language.
Input: “He told a teacher to fuck off” → Output:  fuck off.” (action preserved, the user is reporting, not directing language at anyone)
Input: “He’s been a nightmare, he told a teacher to fuck off” → Output: “He’s been difficult, he told a teacher to fuck off.” (descriptive tone softened, action preserved)
NOT: “He told a teacher to leave him alone” (this changes what happened)
